Second, translate accounts into workload and coverage value. Estimate hospital workload from institution tier, departments, expected call cadence, and preparation time. Connect pharmacies to RFM-pharmacy to distinguish repeat buyers, growth accounts, and recently quiet stores. For chains, consider both head-office coordination and outlet-level movement; for independents, count the extra travel and coordination without dismissing them just for being small. Tier and historical spend are not automatically current opportunity—sales, KA, and product teams should validate those assumptions together.

Third, let K-means draft several candidate territories, then review real constraints. Can each rep absorb visits and preparation time? Does every priority account have an owner and cadence? Should a chain have one point of contact? Can cross-river calls be grouped into a route? For boundary accounts, compare travel saved, coverage value lost, and the receiving territory’s added load instead of arguing over map colors.

Before rollout, compare scenarios using real routes and CRM records: total travel time, priority-account coverage, workload balance, and chain handoffs. Pilot for one cycle, then review completed calls, account feedback, and supply or sell-through goals. The deliverable is not just a map; it includes account owners, suggested cadence, route windows, exception rules, and review measures.
